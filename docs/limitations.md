# Limitations

## The cohort is hand-picked

The 43 prospects were chosen by hand, not drawn systematically (for example, as every top-100 prospect in a period). Results describe these players. The organizations in the cohort have three to six players each, which is too few, and too selectively chosen, to compare organizations, so the project does not.

## Samples are small where it matters most

The prospect analyses rest on 35 players with 850+ MLB plate appearances. Their confidence intervals are wide enough that modest real effects in either direction cannot be ruled out. A null result here means "not supported by this sample," not "proven irrelevant."

## Many metrics, few players

Notebook 03 tests ten early signals. At that count, one or two results near p = .05 are expected by chance, so an isolated borderline result is not reported as a finding.

## Public data cannot explain development

Statcast and FanGraphs show outcomes and approach. They do not show health, coaching plans, mechanical changes, role, or internal evaluations. The case study in notebook 04 describes each player's trajectory; it does not attribute it to decisions by the player or the organization.

## Roster context comes from transaction records

When a player is missing from the data, Statcast does not say why. Absences are read from the official MLB transaction log, which records injured-list placements and options but not the reasons behind options.

## Metric systems disagree

OAA and DRS measure fielding differently and correlate at r = .61 across 300 team-seasons (2017–2026), with 33 team-seasons where they disagree in sign by 10+ runs or outs each. Defensive statements in notebook 05 cite both systems.

## Data coverage

- FanGraphs does not return UBR for 2025–2026, so baserunning for those seasons is reported as BsR and wSB only.
- 2020 was a 60-game season; its counting totals are not comparable with other seasons and it is excluded from the year-to-year analysis.
- Computed plate-appearance totals can run a few below official totals when Statcast is missing a pitch record.
- Minor-league discipline is represented only by official walk and strikeout rates; pitch-level minor-league data is not used.
